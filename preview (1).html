About BATI.BAT

BATI.BAT is a creative craft brand rooted in Filipino identity, mythology, and introspective storytelling. The name is derived from the Philippine mythological creature Batibat, often described in folklore as a demon associated with sleep paralysis. In traditional narratives, the Batibat is feared—appearing in moments between sleep and waking, where dreams blur with reality.

The choice of the name BATI.BAT is intentional. It reflects the visual language and emotional depth that the brand aims to convey through its crafts. Rather than portraying the Batibat solely as a figure of fear, the brand reinterprets it as a symbol of the subconscious—where imagination, darkness, vulnerability, and creativity coexist.

The artistic direction is heavily influenced by Philippine mythology and personal dreamscapes, particularly nightmares. Many creative visions behind BATI.BAT originate from these intense, surreal experiences. Instead of treating nightmares as something to be feared, the brand embraces them as a source of inspiration. BATI.BAT transforms unsettling imagery into meaningful, handcrafted pieces—turning darkness into beauty and fear into form.

About the Artist

Hi! I’m Aesa, a 23-year-old local artist from Metro Manila. I’ve always loved expressing my thoughts and ideas through creativity. I write, draw, make films, crochet, clay-sculpt, and I’m currently exploring sewing.

I first started my shop back in 2022 under the name “Moirae,” where I sold crochet pieces alongside a small baking business I shared with my sister. Most of my hobbies began simply because I wanted to create handmade gifts for the people I love. Gift-giving has always been one of my love languages, and I wanted every piece to feel personal and meaningful.

Eventually, my a little too much art supply hoarding led me to start accepting crochet commissions. It became a way for me to improve my skills while sharing my work with others. Over the years, I made and sold many pieces, but I also found myself repeatedly closing and reopening my shop. At first, I thought it was only because I was busy as a college student, but later I realized that constantly working on commissions started to feel creatively draining. While I enjoyed making them, I often felt disconnected from my own ideas and artistic voice.

Because of that, I began thinking about rebranding in 2025. I was hesitant at first because I worried that people might not appreciate my own art and designs. Even so, I promised myself that by 2026, I would finally learn sculpting and sewing — two things I had always wanted to explore.

And somehow, that promise led me here: Bati.bat!

The heart and inspiration behind the brand can be found in the “About bati.bat” section, but at its core, this shop is a space where I can finally create freely, experiment fearlessly, and share pieces that genuinely come from me.
