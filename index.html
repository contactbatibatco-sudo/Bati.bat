About BATI.BAT

BATI.BAT is a creative craft brand rooted in Filipino identity, mythology, and introspective storytelling. The name is derived from the Philippine mythological creature Batibat, often described in folklore as a demon associated with sleep paralysis. In traditional narratives, the Batibat is feared—appearing in moments between sleep and waking, where dreams blur with reality.

The name comes from Batibat, a creature from Philippine folklore associated with sleep paralysis and the strange space between dreaming and waking. Rather than portraying the Batibat solely as something frightening, BATI.BAT reimagines it as a symbol of the subconscious—where imagination, darkness, vulnerability, curiosity, and creativity meet.

The things I create and curate are inspired by these ideas. Some pieces are handmade by me, while others are carefully sourced or selected because they fit the world, mood, or stories behind BATI.BAT. Rather than limiting the shop to one type of product, I want it to be a space where different objects, artworks, collectibles, and creative finds can exist together.

Much of BATI.BAT's visual direction comes from Philippine mythology and my own experiences with dreams, particularly the strange and surreal imagery that comes from nightmares. I like taking things that might initially feel unsettling, unfamiliar, or strange and turning them into something playful, beautiful, meaningful, or simply worth keeping.

BATI.BAT is ultimately a collection of things I find interesting enough to share—whether I made them myself or found them somewhere along the way.

About the Artist

Hi! I'm Aesa, a 23-year-old artist from Metro Manila.

I started my first small shop in 2022 under the name Moirae, where I sold crochet pieces alongside a small baking business I shared with my sister. It began quite simply—I wanted to make things for the people I cared about and, eventually, share them with others.

As the years went by, I began taking on commissions and selling more of my work. I was grateful for the opportunity to make things that people enjoyed, but balancing the shop with college eventually became overwhelming. I also realized that working mostly on commissions left me with less time and space to explore my own ideas.

This led me to rethink what I wanted a shop to be. Rather than limiting myself to one kind of product, I wanted to create a space that could grow and change with my interests.

That became BATI.BAT.

BATI.BAT is a personal creative shop inspired by Filipino mythology, folklore, dreams, and the things that capture my imagination. Some pieces are made by me, while others are sourced or carefully chosen because I feel they belong in the world I am building with the shop.

I don't want BATI.BAT to be limited to a single medium or category. I simply want it to be a place where I can share things I find meaningful, interesting, strange, or beautiful.

It is a small, one-person shop, and I hope it can grow naturally as I do.
